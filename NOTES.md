# Notes — why this excuse

Entry: "It was blank, it smelled like pencil, and honestly it was the only homework in the house that never once threw a ball for me."

Why this one, against the judge's criteria in order:

1. **An excuse a dog gives, in the dog's own voice.** The speaker is
   the dog, caught in the act, explaining itself — not a person
   narrating a dog.
2. **A twist of dog logic.** The excuse is not "it tasted good" but a
   grievance: the homework never threw a ball, so the dog owed it
   nothing. The last clause is the laugh.
3. **Sounds like a real dog.** Dog priorities only: smell, blankness,
   and above all the ball. No embarrassment, no apology, no human
   guilt — the dog believes it has done nothing wrong.
4. **Works on its own.** One line, no setup, nothing that needs a
   second line to make sense.
5. **Fresh.** The brief warns against the stock lines — the cat, the
   taste of paper, being a good boy. This one uses none of them; the
   "never threw a ball" angle is the dog's own reasoning, not a meme.

Deliberately avoided: "the cat did it", "it tasted like chicken",
and "I'm a good boy, I swear" — the three most crowded excuses in
the field.
